# Lesson: Developing the Introduction of an Oral Presentation

# 1. Greeting the Audience



## Why Is the Greeting Important?

The greeting signals that the presentation is about to begin. According to the guide, these first words also give the speaker an opportunity to establish a position in front of the audience and adjust the voice to the room. 

Useful expressions include:

- **Good morning, ladies and gentlemen.**
- **Good afternoon, everyone.**
- **Good morning. It's a pleasure to be here with you today.**
- **Good afternoon. If everybody's ready, I think we can begin now.**

### Chemical Engineering Example

> **Good morning, everyone. It's a pleasure to be here with you today. Today, I'll be talking about shell-and-tube heat exchangers.**

For a classroom presentation, a simple opening is sufficient:

> **Good morning, everyone. Today, I'm going to talk about plate heat exchangers.**

# 2. Introducing Oneself and the Company


In a professional presentation, the speaker can briefly identify the company and its principal activities. 

Useful expressions include:

- **Let me introduce myself first. I'm...**
- **I'd like to introduce myself. I'm...**
- **Before I begin, let me tell you a little about myself.**
- **I work for...**
- **I'm with...**

### Chemical Engineering Student Example


> **First, I'd like to introduce myself. I'm Ana López, and I'm a Chemical Engineering student.**

Or:

> **Before I begin, let me introduce myself. My name is Carlos Martínez, and I'm currently studying Chemical Engineering.**


> **I'm a process engineer with [company name], where I work in the [department] department.**

# 3. Explaining the Purpose of the Presentation



After the greeting and introduction, the audience should understand **why the presentation is being given**.

Useful expressions include:

- **My purpose today is to...**
- **What I want to do today is to...**
- **My objective today is to...**
- **I'm here today to...**
- **My talk today will deal with...**
- **My presentation will focus primarily on...**

For a technical presentation, the verb following these expressions should indicate what the speaker intends to accomplish.

### Examples:

> **My purpose today is to explain the basic operation of a shell-and-tube heat exchanger and describe its main components.**


> **My presentation today will focus on the operating principle and applications of plate heat exchangers.**


> **My objective today is to describe how a condenser operates and explain its role as heat-transfer equipment.**


> **I'm here today to explain the basic operation of an evaporator and discuss its applications.**

These examples adapt the presentation structures from the guide to the students' assigned heat-transfer equipment; the specific engineering content is not supplied by the guide itself.

### Selecting an Appropriate Verb

Different verbs can communicate different presentation purposes:

| Purpose | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Give information | **present** | I'll **present** the main characteristics of the equipment. |
| Give details | **describe** | I'll **describe** its main components. |
| Make something understandable | **explain** | I'll **explain** how the equipment operates. |
| Study something carefully | **examine** | We'll **examine** its main features. |
| Consider similarities/differences | **compare** | I'll **compare** two operating configurations. |
| Focus attention | **highlight** | I'll **highlight** its main advantages. |

# 4. Giving an Overview of the Presentation



## What Is an Overview?

An **overview** tells the audience what will be discussed and in what order.

A simple structure is:

**First → Then → After that → Finally**

Other sequencing expressions include:

| Beginning | Middle | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| **First of all** | **Next** | **Finally** |
| **To begin with** | **Then** | **Last of all** |
| **To start with** | **After that** | **In the final part** |
| **Firstly** | **Subsequently** | **Finally** |



### Example: Heat Exchanger Presentation

> **First, I'll describe the main components of the heat exchanger. Then, I'll explain how the equipment operates. After that, I'll discuss some of its applications. Finally, I'll look at its main advantages and limitations.**

This gives the audience a clear roadmap:

**Components → Operation → Applications → Advantages and limitations**

### Longer Example

> **To begin with, I'll briefly describe the equipment and identify its main components. Next, I'll explain its operating principle. After that, I'll present some of its applications. Finally, I'll discuss its main advantages and limitations.**

Combine sequencing expressions with verbs that indicate what will happen in each section.

# Putting the Introduction Together

A complete introduction can follow this pattern:

**Greeting → Self-introduction → Purpose → Overview**

### Model Introduction: Shell-and-Tube Heat Exchanger

> **Good morning, everyone. It's a pleasure to be here with you today.**
>
> **First, I'd like to introduce myself. My name is [name], and I'm a Chemical Engineering student.**
>
> **My purpose today is to explain the basic operation of a shell-and-tube heat exchanger and present its main characteristics.**
>
> **First, I'll describe the equipment and its main components. Then, I'll explain its operating principle. After that, I'll discuss some of its applications. Finally, I'll look at its main advantages and limitations.**

# Final Speaking Task

Prepare a **one-minute introduction** for the individual presentation on the assigned heat-transfer equipment.

The introduction should contain all four elements:

1. **Greet the audience.**
2. **Introduce oneself.**
3. **State the purpose of the presentation.**
4. **Give an overview of the presentation.**

Use at least **three sequencing expressions**, such as *first, then, after that,* and *finally*.

# General Summary

* **Greeting the audience** signals the beginning of the presentation and establishes initial contact. 

* **Introducing oneself** gives the audience the relevant identity or professional context of the speaker. 

* **Explaining the purpose** tells listeners exactly what the presentation intends to accomplish.

* **Giving an overview** provides a verbal map that allows the audience to anticipate and follow the organization of the technical presentation.
