# 📘 Main Content

## Introduction to Separation Processes ⚙️

Separation processes are essential in chemical engineering because most industrial streams contain mixtures rather than pure substances. Engineers use different techniques to separate, purify, or recover valuable components from these mixtures.

The choice of separation method depends on the properties of the substances involved, such as:

- 🌡️ Boiling point
- 💧 Solubility
- ⚖️ Density
- 🧪 Chemical affinity
- 🌊 Volatility

This lesson focuses on **distillation** and **extraction**, two of the most important separation processes used in chemical plants around the world.

💡 **Inferred explanation:** Selecting the most appropriate separation method improves product quality, increases process efficiency, and reduces energy and operating costs.

---

# 🌡️ Distillation

**Distillation** is a separation process that separates liquid mixtures based on differences in their **boiling points** or **volatility**.

When a liquid mixture is heated:

1. The more volatile component vaporizes first.
2. The vapor is separated from the remaining liquid.
3. The vapor is cooled.
4. The vapor condenses back into a liquid.
5. The condensed liquid is collected as a purified product.

Distillation is one of the most widely used operations in the chemical and petroleum industries.

---

# ⚙️ How Distillation Works

The basic steps of distillation are:

1. Heat the liquid mixture.
2. Produce vapor rich in the more volatile component.
3. Transfer the vapor to a condenser.
4. Cool the vapor until it condenses.
5. Collect the purified liquid.

The remaining liquid in the distillation vessel becomes richer in the less volatile component.

---

# 🌡️ Boiling Point and Volatility

The effectiveness of distillation depends on the difference in boiling points between the components.

- **Boiling point** is the temperature at which a liquid changes into a vapor.
- **Volatility** describes how easily a substance vaporizes.

A substance with a lower boiling point is generally more volatile.

Example:

Ethanol has a lower boiling point than water, so it vaporizes more readily under the same conditions.

---

# 🏗️ Main Components of a Distillation System

A typical distillation system includes:

- Reboiler
- Distillation column
- Trays or packing
- Condenser
- Reflux drum
- Product outlets

Each component contributes to the efficient separation of the mixture.

---

# 🏢 Distillation Column

The **distillation column** is the main piece of equipment where repeated vaporization and condensation occur.

Inside the column:

- Rising vapor contacts descending liquid.
- Heat and mass transfer occur.
- The vapor becomes richer in the more volatile component.
- The liquid becomes richer in the less volatile component.

This repeated contact increases separation efficiency.

---

# 🔄 Reflux

**Reflux** is the portion of condensed liquid that is returned to the top of the distillation column.

Reflux improves separation by increasing contact between vapor and liquid.

Higher reflux generally produces better separation, although it also increases energy consumption.

---

# 📊 Types of Distillation

Several types of distillation are used in industry.

---

## 🔥 Simple Distillation

Used when the boiling points of the components differ significantly.

Applications:

- Laboratory separations
- Solvent recovery

---

## 🏭 Fractional Distillation

Uses a distillation column to separate mixtures with relatively close boiling points.

Applications:

- Petroleum refining
- Chemical manufacturing
- Air separation

---

## 🌡️ Vacuum Distillation

Operates at reduced pressure to lower boiling temperatures.

Applications:

- Heat-sensitive materials
- Heavy petroleum fractions
- Lubricating oil production

---

# 💧 Extraction

**Extraction** is a separation process in which one component is transferred from one phase to another using a **solvent**.

The solvent selectively dissolves the desired component while leaving other substances behind.

Extraction is widely used when distillation is difficult or inefficient.

---

# ⚙️ How Extraction Works

The basic steps are:

1. Mix the feed with a suitable solvent.
2. The desired component dissolves in the solvent.
3. The two phases separate.
4. The solvent containing the desired component is collected.
5. The product is recovered from the solvent.

The solvent may be reused after product recovery.

---

# 🧪 Solvents

A **solvent** is the liquid used to dissolve and extract the desired component.

A good extraction solvent should:

- Dissolve the target substance effectively.
- Be chemically stable.
- Be easy to recover.
- Be safe to handle.
- Have low toxicity when possible.

The choice of solvent has a major effect on extraction efficiency.

---

# 🌊 Liquid-Liquid Extraction

**Liquid-liquid extraction** involves two immiscible liquids.

One liquid contains the substance to be removed, while the other acts as the solvent.

After mixing:

- The desired component transfers into the solvent.
- The two liquid phases separate because they do not mix completely.

Applications include:

- Pharmaceutical production
- Chemical purification
- Metal recovery

---

# 🌿 Solid-Liquid Extraction

**Solid-liquid extraction** removes soluble components from solid materials.

Examples include:

- Extracting sugar from sugar beets
- Producing vegetable oils
- Preparing coffee and tea

This process is also known as **leaching** in some industries.

---

# ⚙️ Equipment Used for Extraction

Common extraction equipment includes:

### 🧪 Mixer-Settler

A mixer combines the fluids, and a settler allows the phases to separate.

---

### 🏗️ Extraction Column

Provides continuous contact between the feed and the solvent.

---

### 🔄 Agitated Tank

Uses mechanical mixing to improve mass transfer.

The equipment selected depends on production capacity and process requirements.

---

# ⚖️ Comparing Distillation and Extraction

| Distillation | Extraction |
|--------------|------------|
| Based on boiling point differences | Based on solubility differences |
| Requires heating | Usually requires a solvent |
| Produces vapor and condensate | Produces two liquid phases or a solid and a liquid |
| Common for volatile mixtures | Useful for heat-sensitive or non-volatile materials |

In many plants, both processes are used together to achieve high product purity.

# 📖 Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Distillation | Separation process based on differences in boiling point or volatility. |
| Extraction | Separation process using a solvent to transfer a component from one phase to another. |
| Boiling Point | Temperature at which a liquid changes into a vapor. |
| Volatility | Tendency of a substance to vaporize. |
| Distillation Column | Equipment where repeated vaporization and condensation occur. |
| Reflux | Portion of condensed liquid returned to the distillation column. |
| Condenser | Equipment that cools vapor into a liquid. |
| Reboiler | Equipment that supplies heat to the bottom of a distillation column. |
| Solvent | Liquid used to dissolve and remove a desired component. |
| Liquid-Liquid Extraction | Extraction between two immiscible liquids. |
| Solid-Liquid Extraction | Extraction of soluble material from a solid using a liquid solvent. |
| Mixer-Settler | Equipment that mixes phases and then allows them to separate. |
| Leaching | Another term for solid-liquid extraction in many industries. |
| Phase Separation | Division of two immiscible phases after mixing. |
| Purification | Removal of impurities to improve product quality. |
