# 📘 Main Content

## Introduction to Numerical Expressions 📐

Chemical engineers work with numbers constantly. Every experiment, process, calculation, and technical report includes numerical expressions that describe measurements, material properties, operating conditions, and engineering results.

Numerical expressions help engineers communicate precise information. Whether describing the concentration of a solution, the pressure inside a reactor, or the efficiency of a process, engineers must present numerical values clearly and accurately.

Technical English uses standardized ways to express numbers so that engineers from different countries can interpret data consistently.

💡 **Inferred explanation:** Understanding numerical expressions is essential for interpreting technical documents, performing engineering calculations, and communicating quantitative information accurately.

---

# 🔟 Numbers in Engineering

Numbers appear in nearly every engineering activity.

Examples include:

- 🌡️ Temperature: **150 °C**
- 📈 Pressure: **8.5 bar**
- ⚖️ Mass: **12.75 kg**
- 💧 Volume: **250.0 L**
- ⚡ Power: **45 kW**
- ⏱️ Time: **2.5 hours**
- 🧪 Concentration: **0.50 mol/L**

Precision is important because even small numerical errors can affect product quality, equipment performance, and process safety.

---

# 🔹 Decimals

A **decimal** represents a value that is not a whole number.

Decimals are commonly used in engineering because measurements are often more precise than whole numbers.

Examples:

| Decimal | Read as |
|---------|---------|
| 0.5 | zero point five |
| 1.25 | one point two five |
| 3.14 | three point one four |
| 12.08 | twelve point zero eight |
| 125.75 | one hundred twenty-five point seven five |

In American English, the decimal separator is a **period (.)**.

Example:

The pressure is **3.75 bar**.

The density is **998.2 kg/m³**.

---

## 📏 Using Decimals in Engineering

Decimals are used to express:

- Temperature
- Pressure
- Flow rate
- Density
- Concentration
- Efficiency
- Product purity

Example:

*A solution has a concentration of **0.25 mol/L**.*

Example:

*The pump delivers **15.8 L/min**.*

Decimals provide greater accuracy than whole numbers.

---

# 🍰 Fractions

A **fraction** represents part of a whole.

Fractions are still used in engineering, although decimals are more common for calculations.

Common fractions include:

| Fraction | Read as |
|----------|----------|
| 1/2 | one-half |
| 1/3 | one-third |
| 1/4 | one-quarter |
| 2/3 | two-thirds |
| 3/4 | three-quarters |
| 5/8 | five-eighths |

Examples:

- The tank is **one-half full**.
- The pipe is **three-quarters of an inch** in diameter.
- Approximately **one-third** of the energy is recovered.

Fractions are frequently encountered in equipment dimensions, construction drawings, and manufacturing specifications.

---

# 🔄 Converting Fractions and Decimals

Engineers often convert fractions into decimals because decimal values are easier to use in calculations.

Examples:

| Fraction | Decimal |
|----------|---------|
| 1/2 | 0.50 |
| 1/4 | 0.25 |
| 3/4 | 0.75 |
| 1/5 | 0.20 |
| 1/8 | 0.125 |
| 5/8 | 0.625 |

Example:

A valve opening of **1/2** corresponds to **0.50** of its full opening.

Using decimals simplifies engineering calculations and computer-based analysis.

---

# 🔬 Scientific Notation

Scientific notation is a way of writing very large or very small numbers using powers of ten.

This notation makes calculations easier and reduces the possibility of writing errors.

The general form is:

**a × 10ⁿ**

where:

- **a** is a number greater than or equal to 1 and less than 10.
- **n** is an integer representing the power of ten.

---

## 📈 Large Numbers

Examples:

| Standard Form | Scientific Notation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| 1,000 | 1 × 10³ |
| 25,000 | 2.5 × 10⁴ |
| 800,000 | 8 × 10⁵ |
| 4,200,000 | 4.2 × 10⁶ |

Example:

*A chemical plant produces **2.5 × 10⁶ kg** of product annually.*

---

## 🔬 Small Numbers

Examples:

| Standard Form | Scientific Notation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| 0.1 | 1 × 10⁻¹ |
| 0.01 | 1 × 10⁻² |
| 0.001 | 1 × 10⁻³ |
| 0.00025 | 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 0.000001 | 1 × 10⁻⁶ |

Example:

*A catalyst particle has a diameter of **5 × 10⁻⁶ m**.*

Scientific notation is widely used in chemistry because many measurements involve microscopic or extremely large values.

---

# ⚗️ Scientific Notation in Chemical Engineering

Scientific notation is common when working with:

- 🧪 Molecular masses
- ⚛️ Atomic dimensions
- 🔬 Particle sizes
- 💨 Gas concentrations
- ⚡ Energy calculations
- 📈 Process simulations
- 🧬 Reaction kinetics

Examples:

- Avogadro's constant ≈ **6.022 × 10²³ particles/mol**
- Hydrogen ion concentration = **1 × 10⁻⁷ mol/L**
- Nanoparticle diameter = **8 × 10⁻⁹ m**

Using scientific notation makes these values easier to read and compare.

# ➕ Mathematical Operations in Technical English

Chemical engineers frequently describe calculations using mathematical expressions.

Common operations include:

| Symbol | Operation |
|---------|-----------|
| + | Plus (addition) |
| − | Minus (subtraction) |
| × | Times (multiplication) |
| ÷ | Divided by (division) |
| = | Equals |
| % | Percent |

Examples:

- Five plus three equals eight.
- Twelve divided by four equals three.
- The process efficiency is **92%**.

Clear mathematical language helps engineers communicate calculations accurately.

---

# 📊 Significant Figures

In engineering, the number of digits reported reflects the precision of a measurement.

Examples:

- 25 m → Two significant figures
- 25.0 m → Three significant figures
- 25.00 m → Four significant figures

Reporting an appropriate number of significant figures improves the reliability of technical calculations and experimental results.

---

# 📄 Numerical Expressions in Engineering Documents

Numerical expressions appear in many technical documents, including:

- 📘 Equipment manuals
- 🧪 Laboratory reports
- 📊 Engineering calculations
- 📈 Process monitoring reports
- 📋 Technical specifications
- 📚 Scientific articles
- ⚙️ Operating procedures

Engineers must read and interpret these values accurately to ensure safe and efficient operation.

---

# 🌍 Importance of Numerical Accuracy

Accurate numerical expressions help engineers:

- Perform reliable calculations.
- Interpret scientific data correctly.
- Compare experimental results.
- Design equipment accurately.
- Optimize industrial processes.
- Improve product quality.
- Ensure workplace safety.

A small numerical mistake can produce incorrect calculations and lead to significant engineering problems.
