# 📘 Main Content

## Introduction to Concentration and Composition ⚗️

In chemical engineering, it is not enough to know **what substances** are present in a process. Engineers must also know **how much** of each substance is present. This information is described using **concentration** and **composition**.

Concentration indicates the amount of a substance in a mixture or solution, while composition describes the relative amounts of all components in the system.

These concepts are essential for:

- 🏭 Process design
- ⚙️ Process control
- 🧪 Laboratory analysis
- 📊 Quality control
- 🌱 Environmental monitoring
- 💊 Pharmaceutical manufacturing
- 🛢️ Petroleum refining
- 🍎 Food processing

Accurate concentration measurements help engineers maintain product quality, improve efficiency, and ensure safe operation.

💡 **Inferred explanation:** Concentration and composition allow engineers to compare mixtures, monitor chemical reactions, and verify that products meet technical specifications.

---

# 💧 What Is Concentration?

**Concentration** describes the amount of a substance (called the **solute**) present in a given amount of a mixture or solution.

Concentrations may be expressed in several different ways depending on the application.

Examples:

- 2 mol/L sodium chloride solution
- 15 g/L glucose solution
- 10% ethanol by volume
- 500 ppm dissolved solids

Different industries use different concentration units depending on accuracy requirements and the type of process.

---

# ⚗️ Molarity

**Molarity** is one of the most common concentration units in chemistry and chemical engineering.

It is defined as:

> **The number of moles of solute per liter of solution.**

Symbol:

**M** or **mol/L**

Formula:

**Molarity = Moles of solute / Volume of solution (L)**

Example:

A solution containing **2 moles** of sodium chloride dissolved to make **1 liter** of solution has a molarity of:

**2 mol/L**

### Engineering Applications

Molarity is commonly used in:

- Laboratory experiments
- Chemical reaction calculations
- Process analysis
- Titration procedures
- Quality control testing

---

# ⚖️ Molality

**Molality** measures the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.

Symbol:

**m**

Formula:

**Molality = Moles of solute / Kilograms of solvent**

Unlike molarity, molality depends on the mass of the solvent rather than the total volume of the solution.

### Why Is Molality Useful?

Volume changes with temperature, but mass remains constant.

For this reason, molality is useful when studying processes involving significant temperature changes.

### Engineering Applications

Molality is commonly used in:

- Thermodynamic calculations
- Freezing-point analysis
- Boiling-point analysis
- Research laboratories

---

# 📊 Mass Fraction

The **mass fraction** describes the fraction of the total mass contributed by one component.

Formula:

**Mass Fraction = Mass of component / Total mass**

The value ranges from **0 to 1**.

Example:

A mixture contains:

- Water = 80 kg
- Salt = 20 kg

Total mass = 100 kg

Mass fraction of salt:

20 / 100 = **0.20**

Mass fraction of water:

80 / 100 = **0.80**

Mass fractions are widely used in industrial material balance calculations.

---

# 💨 Mole Fraction

The **mole fraction** describes the proportion of the total number of moles contributed by one component.

Formula:

**Mole Fraction = Moles of component / Total moles**

The sum of all mole fractions in a mixture equals **1**.

Example:

A gas mixture contains:

- Nitrogen = 7 mol
- Oxygen = 3 mol

Total moles = 10 mol

Mole fraction of nitrogen:

7 / 10 = **0.70**

Mole fraction of oxygen:

3 / 10 = **0.30**

Mole fractions are commonly used in gas-phase calculations and equilibrium studies.

---

# 📦 Volume Fraction

The **volume fraction** expresses the portion of the total volume occupied by one component.

Formula:

**Volume Fraction = Volume of component / Total volume**

Example:

A mixture contains:

- Ethanol = 30 L
- Water = 70 L

Total volume = 100 L

Volume fraction of ethanol:

30 / 100 = **0.30**

Volume fractions are often used when working with liquid mixtures.

---

# 📈 Percentage Concentrations

Concentrations are frequently expressed as percentages.

Common forms include:

### ⚖️ Mass Percent (% w/w)

Mass of solute per 100 units of total mass.

Example:

10 g salt in 100 g solution = **10% w/w**

---

### 💧 Volume Percent (% v/v)

Volume of solute per 100 units of solution volume.

Example:

70 mL ethanol in 100 mL solution = **70% v/v**

---

### ⚖️💧 Mass/Volume Percent (% w/v)

Mass of solute per 100 mL of solution.

Example:

5 g glucose in 100 mL solution = **5% w/v**

Different percentage expressions are selected according to industry standards and the type of material being measured.

---

# 🔬 Parts Per Million (ppm)

Very dilute solutions are often expressed in **parts per million (ppm)**.

One ppm represents:

**1 part of solute per 1,000,000 parts of solution.**

Examples:

- Drinking water analysis
- Air pollution monitoring
- Wastewater treatment
- Trace metal analysis

Example:

A water sample contains **3 ppm** of dissolved iron.

# 📖 Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Concentration | Amount of a substance in a mixture or solution. |
| Composition | Relative amounts of all components in a system. |
| Molarity | Moles of solute per liter of solution. |
| Molality | Moles of solute per kilogram of solvent. |
| Mole | SI unit for the amount of substance. |
| Solute | Substance dissolved in a solvent. |
| Solvent | Substance that dissolves the solute. |
| Mass Fraction | Mass of one component divided by the total mass. |
| Mole Fraction | Moles of one component divided by the total moles. |
| Volume Fraction | Volume of one component divided by the total volume. |
| Percent Concentration | Concentration expressed as a percentage. |
| ppm | Parts per million, used for very dilute concentrations. |
| Dilute | Containing a relatively small amount of solute. |
| Solution | Homogeneous mixture of solute and solvent. |
| Composition Analysis | Determination of the components and their proportions in a mixture. |
